On Learning Pure Mathematics

January 5, 2026 · 3 min read

Learning pure mathematics is an encounter with abstraction. At first, definitions appear artificial, proofs seem technical, and structure feels distant. Over time, however, patterns emerge.

Consider the classical identity $$\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n^2} = \frac{\pi^2}{6}.$$ What appears as a simple analytic statement encodes deep structural relationships between infinite series and geometry.

On Abstraction

Abstraction in mathematics is not removal of meaning; it is concentration of structure. When we define a group, we are not discarding content — we are isolating symmetry.

On Rigor

Rigor is often misunderstood as pedantry. In reality, rigor is intellectual honesty. It is the discipline of ensuring that every inference follows from clear premises.

Over time, the apparent austerity of pure mathematics reveals a certain elegance — a minimalism of thought.
